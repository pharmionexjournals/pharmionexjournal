Editorial policies · framework for adoption

Trust is part of
the research.

Transparent procedures for fair assessment, responsible research, and a reliable scholarly record. This framework is informed by established publication-ethics principles, including COPE guidance; Pharmionex does not claim COPE membership or endorsement.

Launch status: These are published policy commitments for the journal's planned workflow. The journal is new; it does not claim external indexing, DOI registration, ISSN assignment, or a track record of completed peer review. Contact details and operational arrangements must be verified before accepting submissions.

01 · Editorial workflow

From submission to publication

StageWhat happens
Receipt & technical checkCheck files, article type, required declarations, completeness, scope and basic formatting. Incomplete submissions may be returned for correction.
Editorial screeningAn editor assesses fit, originality, reporting clarity, methodological credibility and ethical concerns. A submission may be declined without external review.
Independent reviewOriginal research, systematic or scoping reviews, meta-analyses, study protocols, methods papers, and case reports are normally assessed by at least two independent external reviewers when suitable reviewers are available. The intended model is double-anonymous review: author identities are removed from the review copy, and reviewer identities are not disclosed to authors. Anonymity cannot be guaranteed; reviewers must declare conflicts if they recognize the work. Editorials, perspectives, and letters may receive editorial assessment, external review, or both according to subject and risk; the route is explained to authors.
DecisionThe handling editor evaluates the reports and makes a reasoned decision. Reviewers advise; editors remain accountable for decisions.
Revision & verificationAuthors submit a clean revision, a marked copy where requested, and a point-by-point response. Revised work may return to reviewers.
Acceptance & productionAccepted manuscripts undergo copyediting, author proof review, metadata checks and final publication preparation.

Review times are not guaranteed while the journal establishes its workflow. Decisions are based on scholarly and ethical merit, not payment or personal relationships.

02 · Peer review

Reviewer guidance

Confidentiality

Treat manuscripts and review materials as confidential. Do not share, quote, upload, or use unpublished material without permission.

Fair, useful assessment

Assess methods, analysis, interpretation, reporting, relevance and ethical safeguards. Explain concerns with evidence and distinguish essential revisions from suggestions.

Conflicts & competence

Accept only work within your expertise and available time. Promptly disclose relationships or interests that could affect impartiality; decline if necessary.

Reviewers should not contact authors directly about a submission. Suspected plagiarism, image concerns, undisclosed conflicts or potential misconduct should be reported confidentially to the editor. Reviewers must not use generative AI services to process confidential manuscript content unless the journal explicitly authorizes a secure, compliant workflow.

03 · Editorial decisions

Decision categories

  • Accept: suitable for publication subject to any stated production requirements.
  • Minor revision: limited changes are needed; the editor may verify the response.
  • Major revision: substantive changes are needed; resubmission does not guarantee acceptance and may be re-reviewed.
  • Reject: the work is unsuitable, unreliable, insufficiently supported, or cannot be made suitable through revision.
  • Decline without review: the manuscript does not meet scope, completeness, quality, or ethical screening requirements.

Authors may appeal once by submitting a concise, evidence-based explanation addressing a material procedural error or substantive misunderstanding. An editor not involved in the original decision should assess the appeal where practicable. An appeal is not a guarantee of reconsideration or acceptance.

04 · Integrity & ethics

Research and publication ethics

01

Plagiarism, duplicate publication & text reuse

Submissions may be screened for overlap. Similarity scores are prompts for contextual editorial assessment, not automatic evidence of misconduct. Properly attributed quotations, methods, preprints and legitimate reuse are assessed in context. Redundant or simultaneous submissions must be disclosed.

02

Fabrication, falsification & image integrity

Fabrication, falsification, deceptive image manipulation, and selective omission that misleads readers are unacceptable. Editors may request underlying records or clarification and may pause review while concerns are assessed.

03

Human participants, animals & consent

Authors must identify the approving ethics committee, institution and approval identifier, or explain a formal exemption. Research must comply with applicable law and institutional standards. State how informed consent was obtained where required. Identifiable details or images require written publication consent; remove unnecessary identifiers.

04

Clinical trials & reporting

Prospectively register applicable clinical trials in a recognized public registry and provide the registry identifier. Explain any prospective-registration exception. Include the relevant reporting checklist and flow diagram where applicable.

05

Authorship & contributor roles

Authors should meet accepted authorship criteria, make a meaningful contribution, approve the final version and accept accountability. Describe individual roles, preferably using CRediT terms. Obtain agreement from all authors before submission; explain changes to authorship and secure written approval from everyone affected.

06

Conflicts of interest

Authors, reviewers and editors disclose financial and non-financial interests that could reasonably be seen to influence the work. Editors with a conflict must recuse themselves and arrange independent handling. Disclose relevant funding and sponsor roles.

07

Generative AI & automated tools

AI tools cannot qualify as authors. Authors remain responsible for accuracy, originality, citations, privacy and permissions. Disclose substantive generative-AI assistance used to create or materially revise manuscript content, including the tool and purpose; routine spelling or grammar correction need not be listed. Do not upload confidential, personal, or unpublished material to tools that may retain or reuse it. Editors and reviewers must protect confidential content and disclose any authorized tool use.

08

Data availability

Include a data-availability statement explaining access to supporting data, code and materials, or why these cannot be shared. Protect participant privacy, consent limits, security and third-party rights. Data should not be made public where doing so would breach law, ethics approval, consent or legitimate confidentiality.

09

Complaints, appeals & investigations

Concerns should be sent to the editorial office with supporting information. The journal will acknowledge and assess complaints fairly, preserve confidentiality where possible, document actions and seek institutional or publisher input when appropriate. Parties may be asked to respond. A concern is not a finding of misconduct.

05 · The published record

Corrections, expressions of concern & retractions

When a credible concern arises, the journal will assess the evidence, contact relevant authors or institutions as appropriate, and take proportionate action. Notices will be linked to the affected article and explain the reason and date. The original record remains identifiable.

  • Correction: for a material error that does not invalidate the work as a whole.
  • Retraction: when findings are unreliable, publication is redundant, or serious ethical or integrity breaches warrant withdrawal.
  • Expression of concern: when a significant investigation is unresolved and readers need an interim notice.

Retractions are not issued solely to punish authors or resolve authorship disputes. Notices should be clear, linked, and preserve the publication history.

06 · Privacy & records

How editorial information is handled

Submission details, manuscript files, declarations, author correspondence, reviewer identities and reports, and editorial case records are used to assess, process, and publish work and to maintain the scholarly record. Operational data is held in restricted Google Forms, Sheets, Drive, and the journal mailbox under the journal account. Manuscript folders should be shared only with the journal and specifically assigned editors or reviewers; public link access must not be enabled for confidential submissions.

Reviewer identities and editor-only comments are kept within the editorial process. The public status checker returns only a general stage and update date after the author supplies the private receipt code. If an article is published, its approved author names, affiliations, article text, references, and publication metadata become part of the public scholarly record.

Authors should not submit unnecessary sensitive personal or patient-identifiable information. The journal uses consumer Google services and email, which are not represented as a dedicated secure manuscript portal. The written retention and deletion schedule is not yet approved; it must be adopted before live manuscript intake. For questions or a request to correct contact information, email pharmionex.journal@gmail.com.
