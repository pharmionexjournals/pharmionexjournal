Editorial policies

Trust is part of
the research.

A policy framework for ethical, transparent and accountable scholarly publishing. Final policies should be reviewed and formally adopted by the journal.

01

Peer review

Submissions should first undergo editorial screening for scope, completeness and basic scholarly standards. Manuscripts selected for review should be evaluated by appropriate reviewers. Reviewers should provide objective, constructive and confidential comments. Review timelines should not be promised until the workflow is operational.

02

Authorship & contributions

Authorship should reflect substantial intellectual or scholarly contributions. All listed authors should approve the submitted manuscript and its final version. Contributors who do not meet authorship criteria may be acknowledged with permission.

03

Research integrity & misconduct

The journal should address concerns involving plagiarism, fabrication, falsification, duplicate publication, image manipulation, unethical research and other serious breaches. Concerns should be documented and investigated using a fair process.

04

Conflicts of interest

Authors, reviewers and editors should disclose relationships or interests that could reasonably be perceived to influence the work or editorial decision. Relevant disclosures should accompany the published record where appropriate.

05

Human & animal research

Manuscripts involving human participants, identifiable information or animals should include appropriate ethical approval or a justified exemption, informed-consent information where applicable, and compliance with relevant standards.

06

Corrections, retractions & updates

Material errors should be corrected transparently. Where a published work is unreliable or seriously compromised, the journal may issue a retraction or other notice. The original record should remain traceable.

07

Plagiarism & similarity

Submitted work may be checked for inappropriate text reuse. Similarity reports are screening tools rather than automatic proof of misconduct; editorial assessment should consider context and source attribution.

08

Copyright & licensing

The journal's final copyright and open-license terms should be stated clearly on each article. Do not claim a specific Creative Commons license until the publisher formally adopts it.

09

Fees & waivers

No author charges are planned for the current zero-budget model. The final fee policy, including whether any future services carry charges, should be published before submissions are accepted.

10

Complaints & appeals

Authors should be able to raise concerns about editorial process or decisions through the journal contact channel. Appeals should provide a reasoned basis and be reviewed independently where practicable.
